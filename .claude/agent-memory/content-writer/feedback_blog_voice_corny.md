Do not reach for weighty, declarative sentences whose only job is to sound serious. The site's own voice (`Hero.astro`, `Features.astro`, `HowItWorks.astro`, `Settings.astro`, `FAQ.tsx`) is short, plain, concrete, second person, and lightly funny without announcing the joke — that register *is* the professional one for this product, not a step down from "corporate."

**Why:** Ryan rejected the first draft of the [[project_v2_launch_post]] as sounding "super corny and pretending to be serious," despite an earlier instruction to make it "polished, corporate, professional." Specific lines that were flagged as offenders and should not resurface in any form: "It isn't a coat of paint." / "A settings screen that respects your time." / "Built for the world, not just English." / "Free, open, and yours." / "Correctness under the real world." / "Measuring the interface instead of eyeballing it." / "the least glamorous kind of audit." / "go watch the game that's actually worth watching." / "one set of compile-time guarantees."

**How to apply:** Concrete test: if a heading states a virtue about the work ("A settings screen that respects your time") rather than what the section is actually about, rewrite it to name the content plainly. If a sentence could be deleted without losing information, it was there for tone — cut it. This applies to the deep-dive/engineering half of a post just as much as the announcement half; technical writing is allowed to be dry, not grand.
