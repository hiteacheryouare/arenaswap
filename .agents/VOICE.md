# VOICE.md

> How ArenaSwap sounds. One voice everywhere, on a spectrum from loose to formal.
> `AGENTS.md` decides what copy is allowed to say. This file decides how it sounds.

---

# Where It Comes From

The voice borrows its warmth from two products and its subject from neither.

- **From Transit:** the joke comes first and a plain fact always follows it. Puns they know are bad. Pride in their home turf (ours is Philadelphia). Thanking users like they're part of the team. Going completely sincere when money, privacy or safety come up.
- **From Slack:** clear, concise, human. Personality never gets in front of what the reader needs. Copy someone reads every day stays plain. The humor switches off when something goes wrong.

Do not borrow their wording, and do not borrow their world. No trains, stops, rides, lines, transfers or commutes. No messages, channels, threads, statuses or workspaces. Our material is the game: the clock, the score, the crowd, the broadcast, the couch and ArenaSwap itself.

- Transit: "Where you are and where you've been can be sensitive information." ([privacy page](https://transitapp.com/privacy))

## Where ArenaSwap Differs

- Transit answers "why pay?" with "We gotta pay the bills!" ArenaSwap moves anything about money to 9 or 10.
- Slack keeps contractions during an outage. ArenaSwap drops them by 10.
- Transit jabs at rival cities. ArenaSwap is openly a Philadelphia fan too, but its jabs stay light and near 0, aimed at a team's history and never at its fans.
- Both write about their own world. Ours is the game.

# The Voice

ArenaSwap sounds like the friend on the couch holding the remote. They're a Philadelphia fan, they know every score, they're quick on the switch, they've got a joke for the commercial break, and when you ask a real question you get a straight answer.

| We are | But never |
|---|---|
| Confident | Cocky, or unsure about a switch |
| Funny | Goofy, or funny at the reader's expense |
| A fan | A hype man |
| Plain | Bland |
| Friendly | Chummy, clingy or needy |
| Smart | Showing off, or explaining the joke |

**Confident.** ArenaSwap is always right (see `AGENTS.md`). Copy states what happened and why: "Switched to Celtics vs. Knicks. Tied with 2:10 left." Never "We think you might like this game." Never apologize for a switch.

**A fan, not a hype man.** Sound like someone who actually watches. Use sports words correctly, and use the right ones for each league: a power play is hockey, a two-minute warning is football. No "epic", "insane", "massive", "next level" or exclamation-point excitement about nothing.

## The Home Team

ArenaSwap is openly a Philadelphia fan: the Eagles, Phillies, Sixers, Flyers and Union. It's built there and doesn't pretend otherwise. Lines already shipped:

- Release notes signed off "(Go Birds 🦅)"
- "Getting a cheesesteak…" (a loading line)
- "go birds" stays "go birds" in every language

Boston can get an occasional friendly nod, since part of ArenaSwap is built there too (the site footer says so). Philadelphia always comes first, and Boston never gets a sign-off of its own.

**A wink, never a requirement.**

- Philly shows up near the loose end only, from about 0 to 3: loading lines, release-note sign-offs, easter eggs. Never in settings, instructions, switch reasons, docs or anything formal.
- One Philly nod per piece, at most. A reader who isn't from Philadelphia loses nothing by not getting it.
- The fandom never touches the product. PowerScore and switching treat every team the same, and copy never suggests otherwise. The only favorite team that counts is the reader's.
- Examples, demo games and screenshots use a mix of teams and leagues. Philly can lead off. It can't be the whole lineup.

**Respect everyone else's team.** Other fan bases' traditions, rituals, songs and players get the same respect we'd want for ours, rivals included. Light jabs at a team's history are fine, and Philly's own history is fair game too. Jabs at a team's fans, its players as people, or its city's people are not.

# Habits That Never Change

These hold everywhere on the spectrum.

- **"We" is ArenaSwap, "you" is the reader.** Talk to one person.
- **The fact behind the joke.** A joke opens or closes a line, and the plain fact is always in the same line or the next one. A line that is only a joke must be skippable without losing anything. An empty-state title can be a joke because its subtitle says what's going on.
- **Specific over sweeping.** Numbers, names and real behaviour. "Checks as often as every 12 seconds" beats "lightning fast."
- **Short sentences carry the point.** Change up the rhythm, then land on a short one.
- **Active voice.** ArenaSwap does things, the reader does things. Nothing "has been enabled."
- **One joke per line, at most.** One joke is a highlight. Five in a row is a blooper reel.
- **Our sources are a network of covert spies with binoculars.** Never name the data provider in prose. Hint at the spies, don't spell out the bit.

# The Spectrum

All copy sits somewhere on one line, from 0 (loosest) to 10 (most formal). It's a slider, not a set of boxes. Any point on it is valid: a release note can sit at 2, a help article at 6, the privacy policy at 9.

```
0          2          4          6          8          10
|----------|----------|----------|----------|----------|
loading    release    popup      help       legal      statements
empty      notes      settings   docs       pages      incidents
404        social     site       eng posts  privacy    security
```

The surface sets where you start. The reader's situation slides it from there (see Sliding, below).

## What Slides

Every part of the voice moves gradually as you slide right. Nothing switches on or off at a line.

| | Near 0 | Near 5 | Near 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jokes** | The line can be the joke, if something next to it carries the fact | One light touch, at the end of a line or in a heading | None |
| **Where the fact sits** | In the next line, or the spinner says it | In the same line | First, before anything else |
| **Puns** | Welcome, one per piece | Gone by 2 or 3 | None |
| **Team ribbing** | Gentle, near 0 only | None | None |
| **Philly nods** | One per piece ("Go Birds 🦅") | Gone by about 3 | None |
| **Sports idioms** | Freely ("Run it back") | Where they're the natural word | None |
| **Exclamation points** | Fine | Rare, announcements only | None |
| **Emoji** | One per line at most | Only as a scanning aid in lists | None |
| **Contractions** | Always | Always | Fade out from about 8 |
| **Sentences** | Short, fragments are fine | Short and complete | Short, complete and declarative |

## Reference Points

These are spots on the line with examples, not categories. Copy that sits between two of them blends the two.

**Around 0.** Loading messages, empty states, the error boundary, the 404 page, easter eggs. Puns, mock-epic drama about small things, absurd stadium details (the hot dog guy, the jumbotron, the mascot), and jokes on ArenaSwap itself. A loading line doesn't need a fact, because the spinner already says "loading." An error still needs a way out. Lines already shipped at this end:

- "Well, that's a turnover." / "Run it back" (the error boundary)
- "The hot dog guy left. 🌭" / "No live action in your leagues. He'll be back when there is."
- "Bribing the refs…"
- "Our scouts are fetching the conference list…"

**Around 2.** Release notes and social posts. The joke goes first and the fact comes right after it:

- "Multiview boxes learned to share. Two boxes on the same streaming site now take turns loading, so each one gets its own game."

**Around 4.** The popup, settings, onboarding, switch reasons and notifications, the website, store listings, READMEs. Plain first, light finish. The line does its job in plain words, and personality, if any, goes at the very end or in a heading, never in the middle of an instruction.

- Instruction stays bare: "Open the popup and pick a game for each tab."
- Personality in the finish: "Turn every league on and ArenaSwap finds the best game on the planet."
- Plain control copy: "Switch Cooldown: the shortest wait between two switches."

Fix this:

- "Notifications have been successfully enabled!" → "You'll get a notification every time ArenaSwap switches."
- "Oops! Something went wrong 😅" → "Couldn't load the scoreboard. Check your connection and try again."
- "Supercharge your game day!" → say what happens on their screen instead.

**Around 6 or 7.** PowerScore docs and the API reference, extension help articles, engineering blog posts, package READMEs, permission justifications. Precise first: definitions, numbers, parameters and steps are exact and dry. Use the glossary terms (Signal, Boost, Penalty) the way `AGENTS.md` defines them, every time. Report numbers straight, and say plainly what a Signal can't see. At most one light touch per section, never inside a definition.

- Definition: "Closeness rises as the margin shrinks and the clock runs down."
- Allowed aside, in a transition: "Halftime scores zero. Nobody needs ArenaSwap to watch the marching band."

Dry is fine here. Grand is not. No "a new paradigm", no "the least glamorous kind of audit", no headline that praises the work instead of naming what the section covers.

**Around 10.** Public statements, outage or incident notices, security and data notices. No contractions, jokes, emoji, exclamation points, sports idioms or spies with binoculars. Short declarative sentences. "We" is still ArenaSwap, but plain and accountable, never chatty. In order:

1. The first paragraph answers: what happened, who is affected, and whether the reader needs to do anything.
2. What we did about it, and when.
3. What changes so it does not happen again, if anything.
4. One apology, if we were at fault. Own it plainly ("We made an error"). Do not hedge, blame a partner, or apologize twice.
5. Where to go with questions.

Example (the incident is made up):

> On October 12, ArenaSwap stopped switching tabs for approximately two hours. Games remained listed, but PowerScores did not update during that time. No user data was affected, and no action is required. Version 4.2.1, released the same evening, resolves the issue and installs automatically. We apologize for the disruption. Questions can be sent to the address on our support page.

Legal pages and the privacy policy sit around 8 or 9: just as exact, but a contraction or a plain "you" is fine when it makes a sentence easier to read.

## Sliding

Start at the surface's spot, then slide:

- **Money, privacy, safety, data, or an apology:** jump to 9 or 10, whatever the surface.
- **The reader is blocked, or missing a game they want:** slide about two toward formal. The error boundary can joke, because it's rare and the button gets them back. A stream that fails to load twice gets plain words and a next step.
- **Seen many times a session:** slide toward 5. A switch reason someone sees forty times a game gets no jokes.
- **Seen once:** a welcome screen or an easter egg can slide one looser.
- **Within one piece, the spot can drift:** a release-note headline at 1 with a body at 3, a help article intro at 4 with steps at 6. Drift between lines, not inside one.

When in doubt, slide toward formal. A dry line that works beats a joke that doesn't.

# Humor

**Fair game:** refs, commissioners, mascots, concessions, the jumbotron, superstitions, fourth-down debates, the GOAT argument, our sources and their binoculars, ArenaSwap's own fumbles, a Philly nod, and gentle team ribbing near 0.

**Team ribbing:** poke at a team's well-known history (droughts, curses, a revolving door at quarterback), rivals and Philadelphia's own included, never at its fans as people, a player's personal life, an injury, or anything off the field. History goes stale, so check it's still true. If the joke only works when the reader's team is losing, cut it.

**Off limits:** the reader and their taste, injuries, real tragedies, a player's personal life, anything off the field, betting (never encourage it), and anything a fan of the joke's target would read as mean.

**Puns:** the loose end only (0 to 2), one per piece, and never on the names ArenaSwap, PowerScore, Multiview or Standby Stream.

**Self-deprecating, about the right things.** ArenaSwap can laugh at itself fumbling a loading screen. It never jokes about whether switching works. That part is serious.

# Punctuation and Emoji

- **Exclamation points and emoji:** follow What Slides. Never two exclamation points in a row, never more than one emoji in a line, and never an emoji in place of a word. The league list in the store description is the model for emoji as a scanning aid.
- **Em dashes:** none. Use a period, a colon or a new sentence.
- **Ellipses:** loading lines use the single `…` character.
- **Contractions:** everywhere until they fade out from about 8.

---

# Words

**Avoid everywhere:** unlock, seamless, elevate, supercharge, game-changer, next level, dive into, leverage, effortless, ninja, rockstar, "it's not just X, it's Y", and any line that could open another product's copy.

**Avoid the inspirations' worlds:** no transit metaphors, no chat or workplace metaphors, no Slack or Transit in-jokes.

**Sport-agnostic by default.** Generic lines use words every sport shares (game, score, clock, the final minutes). Save league-specific words for copy about that league.

# Before You Ship a Line

1. Where on the spectrum does this sit, and does every row of What Slides match that spot?
2. Would the reader lose anything they need if this line were deleted? If not, delete it (`AGENTS.md`, Copy Mistakes to Avoid).
3. Is the plain fact still there, next to the joke?
4. Is every number and claim verified against the code?
5. Read it out loud. Does it sound like the friend on the couch, or like a brochure?
